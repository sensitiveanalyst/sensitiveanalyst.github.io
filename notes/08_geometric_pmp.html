08 Geometric PMP on manifolds and Lie groups

Status. Implemented and tested: rigid-body attitude on SO(3) x R^3 in geometric_pmp.py. Sections marked sketch are not implemented. Claims marked (literature) are recalled from memory; check the reference before citing.

1. Coordinate-free statement

M a smooth manifold, x' = f(x,u). Lift to the cotangent bundle: for xi = (x, lam) in T*M set H_u(xi) = <lam, f(x,u)> + lam0 L(x,u), with the canonical symplectic form. An extremal is a curve xi(t) with xi' = X_{H_u(t)}(xi) (Hamiltonian vector field), H_u(t)(xi(t)) = min_v H_v(xi(t)), and lam(T) annihilating the tangent space of the terminal set (plus lam0 d phi). No charts, no Euler angles, no unit-norm constraint, no angle wrapping. (literature: Agrachev and Sachkov, Control Theory from the Geometric Viewpoint.)

2. Lie group, left-invariant problem

Trivialise T*G = G x g*, (g, mu). For g' = g xi(u) and cost L(u):

H(g, mu, u) = <mu, xi(u)> + L(u)          does not depend on g
mu' = ad*_{xi*} mu        xi* = dH/dmu    Lie-Poisson equation on g*
g'  = g xi*                               reconstruction on G

H and the Casimirs (for so(3): |mu|^2) are conserved; the flow stays on a coadjoint orbit. For so(3), ad*_xi mu = mu x xi, which is Euler's equation Pi' = Pi x W. First-order example: H = |u|^2/2 + mu . u gives u* = -mu, mu' = 0, so xi is constant and g = g0 exp(t xi): geodesics are one-parameter subgroups.

3. The implemented case: attitude with torque input

State (R, W), J W' = (J W) x W + u, cost (r/2)|u|^2 or a smooth torque bound. Costates (Pi, p): Pi for R (in so(3)*), p for W. After reducing by the left SO(3) action the closed 9-dimensional system (derived by hand, tested numerically) is

W'  = J^-1 ((J W) x W + u*)        u* = -J^-1 p / r
Pi' = Pi x W                       Lie-Poisson part
p'  = -Pi + (J W) x g - J (W x g)  g = J^-1 p, canonical part

R drops out, so shooting works on (W, Pi, p) and R is rebuilt afterwards. For J = j I the cross terms cancel: p' = -Pi, hence W''' = W'' x W, i.e. the Riemannian cubic equation v''' + [v, v''] = 0 of the bi-invariant metric. (literature: Noakes, Heinzinger, Paden 1989; Crouch and Silva Leite 1995.) I checked by hand that the covariant derivation and this one agree.

4. Why the tests look the way they do

Constant H and met boundary conditions do not validate Pi' = Pi x W: flipping its sign still gives a constant H and a solution that hits the target. The tests therefore add an independent first-order check: perturb the optimal torque with 18 smooth modes, differentiate cost and terminal error by finite differences, and require the cost gradient to lie in the span of the terminal-error gradients (the KKT condition). - correct extremal: relative residual about 4e-10 - feasible cubic-in-time baseline (cost 19% higher): 0.37 - extremal with the wrong sign of Pi': 0.42

Other checks: W' = dH/dp and p' = -dH/dW by finite differences, Casimir conservation, the cubic identity for spherical J, left-invariance (rotate both endpoints by Q, same cost and body rates), the small-angle double-integrator closed form, and fourth-order convergence of the Magnus step.

5. Reconstruction and discretisation

Fourth-order Magnus: R_{k+1} = R_k exp(h/2 (W1 + W2) + (sqrt3 h^2/12) W1 x W2) with Gauss points W1, W2. Stays on SO(3) to machine precision. Alternatives: RKMK, Crouch-Grossman. Sketch: a variational integrator on TG gives discrete extremals that keep the symplectic structure; compare with the continuous solution. (literature: Bloch, Hussein, Leok, Sanyal 2009, geometric structure-preserving optimal control of a rigid body.)*

6. SE(3) (sketch, hand-derived from ad, not coded or tested)*

For xi = (W, v) and mu = (Pi, P) in se(3)*:

Pi' = Pi x W + P x v          P' = P x W          Casimirs: |P|^2, Pi . P

Kinematic minimum-effort problems on SE(3) reduce to these. The quadrotor with thrust depends on R through R e3, so it is not left-invariant; reduce by the stabiliser (translations and yaw) or use differential flatness (note 10).

7. Sub-Riemannian and nonholonomic problems (sketch, literature)

Distribution D, H = |lam|_D^2 / 2. Normal extremals project to geodesics; abnormal extremals exist and may be minimisers (Montgomery, A Tour of Subriemannian Geometries). Curvature of the connection defining D is what makes motion by cycling controls possible (note 10).
