10 Flat connections, Lie algebras and characteristics

Status. Outline only; no code, except that geometric_pmp.py is an instance of sections 1 and 4. Statements I derived by hand are marked (derived); (literature) means recalled from memory, so check before citing.

Two meanings of flat. Differential flatness (outputs from which states and inputs are algebraic functions, Fliess et al. 1995) turns the quadrotor into four chains of integrators in (x, yaw) and makes minimum-snap polynomial (literature: Mellinger and Kumar). A flat connection is a connection with zero curvature. This note is about the second.

1. Connections on a Lie group (derived)

Maurer-Cartan form theta = g^-1 dg satisfies d theta + (1/2)[theta, theta] = 0. Three connections on left-invariant fields X, Y, Z: | Connection | nabla_X Y | Torsion | Curvature | |------------|-------------|---------|-----------| | (-) Cartan-Schouten | 0 | -[X,Y] | 0 (flat) | | (+) | [X,Y] | [X,Y] | 0 (flat) | | (0) Levi-Civita for a bi-invariant metric | (1/2)[X,Y] | 0 | R(X,Y)Z = -(1/4)[[X,Y],Z] |

Consequences for control: - No holonomy under (-). Parallel transport is left translation, so left-trivialised velocities and costates at different times can be compared directly. That is why the PMP reduces to ODEs on g* and the group appears only in the reconstruction. - Covariant derivative under (0). D_t W = W' + (1/2)[xi, W]. Using it, the Riemannian cubic equation D^3 v + R(Dv, v)v = 0 becomes v''' + [v, v''] = 0, the same equation the reduced PMP gives for a spherical body (note 08). - Curvature and conjugate points. <R(X,Y)Y, X> = (1/4)|[X,Y]|^2 >= 0: SO(3) with the metric |xi| = |rotation vector| has constant sectional curvature 1/4 (it is RP^3 of radius 2). Jacobi fields oscillate, the first conjugate point of a geodesic is at rotation angle 2 pi, and the cut locus of the identity is the set of rotations by pi, where log_so3 is singular. Extremals that wrap past it stop being optimal.

2. Zero curvature as integrability

g^-1 dg = A has a solution exactly when dA + (1/2)[A, A] = 0. - Time only (1-D base). Always satisfied, so R' = R hat(W) always integrates; the Magnus step in the code is a solver for that fact. - Space and time (2-D base). For R_s = R hat(u), R_t = R hat(w) the condition is u_t - w_s = u x w (derived). It couples the controls along a rod, continuum robot or flexible structure, and the adjoint of a PDE-constrained PMP has the same form. Sketch. - Lax form. An extremal flow that can be written L' = [L, M] has the isospectral invariants as first integrals; with a spectral parameter that is a zero-curvature condition. Examples: Manakov's rigid body on so(n), elastica on SE(2) (literature: Jurdjevic, Geometric Control Theory). Use: extra invariants to test the solver, and fewer shooting unknowns when the flow is Liouville integrable.

3. Curvature as control authority (literature, sketch)

For a distribution given by a connection, the curvature is the Lie bracket of horizontal fields. Net motion from a closed loop in the base is proportional to enclosed area times curvature (Wong's equations, falling cat, parking a car). Flat means no motion from cycling controls. Chow-Rashevskii: brackets spanning the tangent space give controllability.

4. Method of characteristics

HJB for the cost-to-go: -V_t = H*(x, grad V), H*(x,p) = min_u [L + p . f], with Cauchy data V(T, .) = phi given at the terminal time.

HJB PMP
p = grad V costate lam
characteristic ODE x' = H*_p, p' = -H*_x Hamiltonian system of the extremal
Cauchy data p(T) = grad phi transversality lam(T) = grad phi
dV/dt = -L along a characteristic (derived) accumulated cost

On a Lie group with left-invariant H the characteristic system is the Lie-Poisson flow plus reconstruction, which is what geometric_pmp.py integrates.

Caustics. Characteristics cross at conjugate points and the cut locus. Past them the smooth solution is multivalued; the value function is the minimum over branches, with a kink where branches meet, and the optimal feedback jumps there. That is a continuity break in the feedback. Two smoothings: - Vanishing viscosity: add e Laplacian(V), equivalent to noise in the dynamics. For x' = u + sigma w with cost |u|^2/2 + q(x), the substitution V = -sigma^2 log Z makes the PDE linear (Hopf-Cole) (derived), so V and the feedback are smooth across the cut locus (literature: Fleming, Kappen path-integral control). - Soften the cost (note 09) so the extremal field has fewer caustics.

Lyapunov by characteristics. grad V . f = -q is a first-order PDE whose characteristics are the trajectories: V(x0) = int_0^inf q(phi_t(x0)) dt (Zubov-style). For linear f this is P = int e^{A^T t} Q e^{A t} dt, the Lyapunov equation lyapunov.py solves.

5. Pipeline: smooth geometric PMP to a feedback (sketch)

1  left-invariant model on G, cost with a smooth bound (barrier)        note 09
2  reduced Hamiltonian system on g* (+ canonical part)                  geometric_pmp.py
3  shooting with continuation in the smoothing width                    pmp.py, geometric_pmp.py
4  reconstruct on G with Magnus                                         geometric_pmp.py
5  verify: boundary conditions, H constant, KKT check, find_breaks on u(t)
6  sample many initial states; store (x, lam, V) along extremals        characteristics
7  fit V_hat(x) to the data; feedback u = argmin_u H(x, grad V_hat, u)  (literature: Nakamura-
                                                                        Zimmerer, Gong, Kang 2021)
8  or neighbouring-extremal gains from the Riccati P(t)                 note 07 section 5
9  close the loop with the estimator                                    note 05
