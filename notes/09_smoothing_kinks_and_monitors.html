09 Smoothing kinks and continuity breaks in monitors

Status. Implemented and tested: smooth.py, continuity.py, the smooth blend in PendulumSwingUp, the smoothed quadrotor normalisations, and the barrier-smoothed PMP in pmp.py / geometric_pmp.py. Items marked sketch are not implemented; (literature) means recalled from memory.

Reading of the request. "Monitoring system" is taken as the runtime signals around a controller: thresholds, mode flags, guards, clipped outputs, and temporal-logic robustness monitors. A continuity break in one of them is a jump (C0) or a kink (C1) in a logged signal. The plan: find the breaks, replace each by a smooth modifier, check again.

Every modifier trades exactness for differentiability: the error is O(width) and derivatives grow like 1/width, so integrators and shooting get stiffer as the width shrinks. Lower it by continuation, not in one step.

3. Smoothing inside the PMP: modify the cost, not the control law

For a quadratic cost r u^2 / 2 and a box |u| <= u_max the minimiser of H is clip(-g/r), where g is the part of dH/du that does not come from L. Instead of replacing the clip by a tanh by hand, choose the input cost

w(u) = (r u_max^2 / 2) [(1+s) ln(1+s) + (1-s) ln(1-s)],   s = u / u_max
w'(u) = r u_max atanh(s)   =>   argmin_u  w(u) + g u  =  -u_max tanh(g / (r u_max))

The tanh law is then the exact Hamiltonian minimiser of a smooth, strictly convex problem, so the minimum principle, the conservation of H and the symplectic structure all hold for the smoothed problem. A tanh patched into the law would break the costate equations. - r -> 0: bang-bang. Large r: quadratic cost r u^2 / 2. - With fixed T and no other cost, r only scales the objective, so it is not a width. It becomes one when time is in the cost (1 + w, free T). Tests rely on that. - Rigorous bracket for the min-time double integrator (tested): T* <= T_eps <= T*(1 + e ln 2), because smoothed controls obey the bound and the bang-bang control is feasible. The attitude analogue (spherical body, rotation about the cube diagonal) has the same bracket with 3 r ln 2. - Continuation: solve at large r, shrink, warm-start each solve. (literature: Bertrand and Epenoy 2002 on smoothing bang-bang problems.) - L1 and fuel cost, state constraints (sketch): replace |u| by smooth_abs, and c(x) <= 0 by a log barrier -e log(-c) so the costate stays continuous.

4. Monitors

Detect. find_breaks(t, y) reports jumps and kinks. A break is whatever the sampling cannot resolve as smooth, so a rounded corner many samples wide is not reported. On a clipped sine it finds each of the 8 kinks once, with slope change 10.4 against the exact 12 cos(pi/6) = 10.39. On the hard-switch pendulum it finds 7 breaks in u(t); with blend_width = 0.15 it finds none and the swing-up still reaches the top.

Replace. - Threshold flag or guard: smooth_switch(s, lo, hi, a, b). Outside the band it equals the old behaviour exactly, which keeps the verified region unchanged. - Convex-blend lemma: for control-affine dynamics and one shared Lyapunov function V, V-dot is affine in u, so a blend of two controllers that each satisfy V-dot <= -gamma V also satisfies it. The pendulum's LQR and energy laws use different V, so that lemma does not cover its blend band; only simulation does. - STL robustness: always is a min over time, eventually a max. Soft versions are differentiable, so MPC and shooting can differentiate through the monitor, and smooth_always >= 0 implies the true robustness is >= 0. (literature: Pant, Abbas, Mangharam, "Smooth Operator", 2017.) - Sliding-mode sign(s): smooth_sign gives a boundary layer. Exact convergence becomes ultimate boundedness of order e. - Hysteresis and dwell time are hybrid by design; smoothing them removes the guarantee they exist to give. Smooth the vector field, keep the logic.

5. Checklist before trusting a smoothed loop

  1. Re-derive the Lyapunov decrease with margin; V-dot <= -gamma V may now hold only outside a band of width O(e).
  2. Smoothing moves equilibria and invariant sets by O(e). Safe sets need one-sided (inner) approximations, as smooth_min provides.
  3. Run find_breaks on the closed-loop signals again.
  4. Sweep the width and look for stiffness or a lost stability margin.

6. What smoothing cannot fix

There is no continuous feedback that globally stabilises a point on SO(3): the topology forces a discontinuity or a bad equilibrium. (literature: Bhat and Bernstein 2000.) geometric.py already accepts the best continuous result, almost-global convergence; smoothing only moves the unavoidable break to a measure-zero set. Brockett's condition gives the same kind of obstruction for nonholonomic systems (literature). Alternatives are hybrid control or time-varying feedback.

7. Quadrotor normalisations (QuadrotorSE3Controller)

smooth_normalize is the wrong tool here: v / sqrt(|v|^2 + e^2) is shorter than 1, so the desired attitude would stop being a rotation matrix and the geometric error would lose its meaning. The fixes keep Rd exactly in SO(3). Both singularities are topological, so they are relocated out of the flight envelope, not removed.

Thrust direction, b3d = -A/|A|. No smooth map from commands to unit vectors can equal -A/|A| outside a ball: restricted to a sphere it would have degree 1 yet extend over the ball. The fix shifts the command first, A_s = ((1+k) A_x, (1+k) A_y, A_z - k m g), k = thrust_eps, and normalises A_s. For an upward thrust command (A . e3 <= 0) A_s is never zero, so b3d is smooth through A = 0 (level attitude there). It is singular only at the downward command A = k m g e3, which needs negative thrust. (derived, tested) - Hover is unchanged: A = -m g e3 gives b3d = e3, f = m g. - The (1+k) on the horizontal part keeps tilt per unit horizontal error equal to the paper's to first order. Without it the tilt is multiplied by 1/(1+k) (measured 0.98039 for k = 0.02) and the step response converges visibly slower. - Away from hover the thrust direction differs by O(k): at the default k = 0.02 the 1.2 m step trajectory stays within 1 cm of the exact controller. - f = -A . R e3 still uses the unshifted A.

Heading, b2d = b3d x b1d / |.|. The paper's frame is singular at b3d = +-b1d, and as the thrust axis passes the heading the frame flips by 180 degrees (a jump of size 2 in the entries of Rd; find_breaks finds it at exactly pi/2). By the hairy-ball theorem every frame field on the sphere has a singularity; this one has two. heading="tilt_twist" uses Rd = Rmin(e3 -> b3d) Rz(psi) with psi = atan2(b1d_y, b1d_x), which has one, at b3d = -e3 (thrust straight down). It is continuous through the heading and out to 3.0 rad of tilt in the sweep test. It needs a heading reference with a horizontal component. - Same as the paper for pure pitch and pure roll (difference 2e-16, tested). - For mixed tilt theta the frames differ by about theta^2/4 (second order, measured).

Defaults changed. thrust_eps=0.02, heading="tilt_twist". thrust_eps=0, heading="projection" reproduces the paper's Rd exactly (tested against a copy of the formula).

Not proven. Lee, Leok and McClamroch's Lyapunov argument assumes the exact b3d. The shift adds a perturbation of order k that vanishes at hover (the equilibrium is unchanged), and simulation shows convergence from a 1 m step and from 120 degrees of initial tilt, but there is no stability proof for the modified controller.
