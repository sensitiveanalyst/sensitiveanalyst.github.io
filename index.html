coh-thread

Terminal functions for high-operation and catastrophe-prevention environments: estimation, stability checks, command limits and monitors that you can call from a terminal or a script.

Free, open, and written to say plainly what each function does, why it matters when failure is costly, and where it stops.

Why this exists

Some systems cannot be restarted after they fail: an aircraft in flight, a process plant, a cooling or power loop, a control room at three in the morning. Much of the tooling built around such systems is built to be faster, cheaper or more profitable. coh-thread is built for the other job: making the unrecoverable event less likely, and saying so honestly when it cannot.

Prevention, not optimisation

We do not optimise for speed, throughput, revenue or engagement. The work is measured by what it helps avoid.

Not for sale

The library is free to read and run, and its source is readable on this site. coh-thread is not selling you anything, and nothing here is built to take money from the people who use it.

Honest about limits

Every entry in the library says where it stops. A tool that overstates what it guarantees is itself a hazard.

How we work

  • Proof and evidence are labelled differently. check_vdot_negative says it can only falsify a stability claim, never prove one. The smoothed quadrotor controller says its stability is checked in simulation, not proven.
  • Failure is loud. lyapunov_equation refuses an unstable system instead of returning a false certificate. PMPShooting.solve raises an error when it does not converge instead of handing back a plausible trajectory.
  • Bounds hold by construction. tanh_sat and barrier_control cannot leave their limits. smooth_always can raise a false alarm, but it cannot call a violated margin safe.
  • The unfinished parts are written down. Each of the 10 theory notes states what it assumes and what is still unresolved.

Where it stands

  • Available
    The control suite. Estimation, stability certificates, geometric control on SO(3) and SE(3), smooth replacements for kinks, discontinuity detection, and Pontryagin-based planning. The source and the theory notes are readable on this site.
  • Planned
    Vision-model support. Terminal functions that bring vision-model output into the same toolkit for operations. No vision code exists in the repository yet.
  • Open question
    What a vision model may be trusted with. A model that sees is also a new way to be wrong. If you run operations with a camera or a vision model in the loop, tell us what it must and must not be allowed to decide.

Read before relying on it

This is research-grade code. Its tests are simulations and known-answer checks. It carries no safety certification and no independent audit, and we do not claim it has been validated on real plants. Use it as one layer among several, as a second opinion, a monitor or a way to test an argument. Never use it as the only barrier between a fault and a catastrophe.

  • Adding or extending terminal functions for your operators' workflow.
  • Vision-model support, once you tell us the use case.
